Ternary phase diagrams for polymer / solvent / non-solvent systems

This page computes Flory-Huggins phase diagrams: the binodal, the spinodal and the tie-lines. Choose a system, change the parameters if you like, and press Compute. Click on the diagram to test a composition. For the two Torlon systems with 2-propanol the parameters are fitted to published cloud points (Ishaque 2020). For the other systems they are rough estimates.

About the data. The only measured data on this page are nine published cloud points for Torlon 4000HV with 2-propanol (Ishaque 2020), shown as squares for the two Torlon systems. Everything else comes from theory and handbook values: the Flory-Huggins model, interaction parameters estimated from published Hansen solubility parameters, and rough estimates for the other systems.

How to read the diagram

Each point in the triangle is one composition, and the three percentages add up to 100. Read each component along its own axis. Its grid lines run parallel to the side opposite its corner.

  • Outside the binodal (red line): one phase. The solution is clear and stable.
  • Between the binodal and the spinodal (blue dashed line): metastable. The solution separates by nucleation and growth of the second phase, which can take time.
  • Inside the spinodal: unstable. The solution separates spontaneously, typically into an interconnected structure (spinodal decomposition).

Inside the grey region a composition splits into two phases. A tie-line joins the two phases that coexist, and the position of a composition along its tie-line gives the share of each phase (the lever rule).

In immersion precipitation, solvent leaves the cast film while non-solvent enters, so the composition moves from the solvent and polymer side toward the polymer and non-solvent side. If it enters the two-phase region at a higher polymer content than the critical point, the polymer-lean phase nucleates, which usually gives a porous, sponge-like structure. Below the critical point the polymer-rich phase nucleates as droplets, which tends to give a weak, nodular structure. In a cloud-point titration, non-solvent is added to a polymer solution, so the composition moves straight toward the non-solvent corner until the solution turns turbid.

Fit to measured cloud points

Cloud points of Torlon 4000HV in NMP and in DMF, titrated with 2-propanol, are given in Ishaque (2020), Figure 36. The points were read off that figure, taken as weight percent, and converted to volume fractions. The model was then fitted to these points. This is a calibration, not an independent test: the model is compared with the same nine points it was fitted to.

Densities at 25 °C, in g/cm³: 2-propanol 0.781, NMP 1.028, DMF 0.944, Torlon about 1.40.

The fit is not unique. Any χ13 from about 1.4 to 1.8 fits within 1 wt % if χ23 is adjusted with it. Over that range χ23 for NMP goes from about 0.46 to below zero, while χ23 for DMF only goes from about 0.52 to 0.44. Two results hold over the whole range. χ13 must be at least about 1.3 to 1.4. And DMF is always the weaker solvent for Torlon, not far from the value at which it would stop dissolving long chains (χ23 = 0.54 for n3 = 600). In the fit χ23 was kept between −0.3 and that limit. These conclusions depend on the Hansen estimates for χ12. An independent measurement, for example the swelling of Torlon in 2-propanol, would be needed to fix the individual values.

The points were read from a figure, so they carry about ±0.5 wt % of reading error. The figure does not state its units. If the source tables are in volume percent, the fit has to be redone.

Method

Flory-Huggins free energy of mixing per lattice site, for non-solvent (1), solvent (2) and polymer (3) with volume fractions φi: 

ΔG_m / RT = φ1 ln φ1 + φ2 ln φ2 + (φ3/n3) ln φ3
          + χ12 φ1 φ2 + χ13 φ1 φ3 + χ23 φ2 φ3

Binodal. Two phases coexist when all three chemical potentials are equal in both. The program starts on the polymer / non-solvent side, where the problem is a binary one, and then follows the binodal into the triangle one tie-line at a time. At each step it fixes the polymer content of the polymer-rich phase and solves the three equations with Newton's method. It works with the logarithms of small fractions, because the polymer-lean phase can contain less than 10−300 polymer. The steps get smaller as the tie-lines get shorter, and the calculation stops where the tie-line shrinks to a point, at the critical point.

Spinodal. The limit of local stability, where the determinant of the second derivatives of ΔGm is zero: 

G22 G33 − G23² = 0
G22 = 1/φ1 + 1/φ2 − 2 χ12
G33 = 1/φ1 + 1/(n3 φ3) − 2 χ13
G23 = 1/φ1 − χ12 − χ13 + χ23

Checks. On every computed tie-line the chemical potentials agree to within about 10−10 per lattice site, and the binodal closes where the spinodal determinant passes through zero, as it must at a critical point. The solver was compared with an independent implementation in Python (SciPy): tie-lines agree to better than 10−7, critical points to about 3 × 10−5, and the fitted cloud points to 0.1 wt %. When you click a point in the two-phase region, the tie-line through it is solved exactly, not interpolated.

Downloads. The three buttons under Download save the current diagram as CSV files, with every composition in wt % and in vol %. The header lines give the system, the parameters and the densities used to convert volume fractions to weight fractions. The binodal file has the polymer-rich branch, the critical point and the polymer-lean branch in order, so it plots as one closed curve. The tie-line file has every computed tie-line, not only the ones drawn. The spinodal file is computed over the whole triangle, whatever part is shown. Each spinodal point is found by bisection on the determinant above, so it lies on the spinodal to rounding error. The checks read the files back: the chemical potentials in the two phases of each tie-line still agree to better than 10−9, and the spinodal passes through the critical point.

Limitations

The χ parameters are constant, with no dependence on composition or temperature. Both small molecules occupy one lattice site, the polymer has a single chain length, and there is no ternary interaction term. These are the textbook assumptions. They work poorly for water-based systems, where the interaction parameters depend strongly on composition. Altena and Smolders, and Romay, Diban and Urtiaga, use a composition-dependent non-solvent / solvent parameter for this reason, and Yilmaz and McHugh show how sensitive the diagram is to the interaction parameters and their concentration dependence. For the water systems in the list, χ13 is set between 2 and 3, which puts the cloud points at a few to about 20 wt % water, the usual order of magnitude. These values are not fitted to data.

The program traces the two-phase region that starts on the polymer / non-solvent side, which is the normal case for membrane casting. It gives a note when the solvent and non-solvent would separate on their own, when the solvent is too poor to dissolve the polymer, or when no two-phase region starts on that side.
